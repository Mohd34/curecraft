## 3. The Salt Offset Equation

Because American Prague Powder #1 is **93.75% common table salt**, every gram of Prague Powder added contributes salt to your meat. Neglecting this causes over-salted, unpalatable charcuterie.

$$\text{Pure Salt Required (g)} = \left(\text{Meat Weight} \times \frac{\text{Target Salt \%}}{100}\right) - (\text{Prague Powder Weight (g)} \times 0.9375)$$

### Example Calculation (1,000g Pork Loin, 2.5% Target Salt, 156.25 PPM Nitrite):
- **Meat Mass:** $1,000\text{ g}$
- **Prague Powder #1:** $1,000 \times 0.0025 = \mathbf{2.50\text{ g}}$
- **Ingoing Nitrite:** $\frac{2.50 \times 0.0625}{1000} \times 1,000,000 = \mathbf{156.25\text{ PPM}}$
- **Salt in Cure #1:** $2.50 \times 0.9375 = 2.34\text{ g}$
- **Pure Salt Needed:** $(1,000 \times 0.025) - 2.34 = 25.00 - 2.34 = \mathbf{22.66\text{ g}}$
- **Total Combined Salt:** $22.66\text{ g (pure salt)} + 2.34\text{ g (from cure)} = \mathbf{25.00\text{ g (exact 2.50\%)}}$.

## 4. Cure #1 vs Cure #2 Application Rules

- **Use Prague Powder #1 ONLY for:**
  - Cured meats that will be cooked or smoked: bacon, ham, pastrami, corned beef, fresh cured sausages (kielbasa, frankfurters).
  - Short equilibrium cures (under 30 days) that do not undergo long dry-aging.
- **Use Prague Powder #2 ONLY for:**
  - Dry-cured, fermented charcuterie requiring extended hanging and aging (>30 to 180+ days): salami, prosciutto, coppa, lonza, culatello.
  - *Mechanism:* Sodium nitrate ($\text{NaNO}_3$) acts as a slow time-release reservoir, breaking down into nitrite ($\text{NaNO}_2$) over months via bacterial nitrate reductase.
- **NEVER use Prague Powder #2 on bacon or meats cooked at high frying heat** because residual nitrates and high heat can accelerate nitrosamine formation.
